# UrbanResearch
Interesting things that happen to include things in the city sphere of life

## Ideas to try next

Under-studied angles, picked because they are awkward to measure or fall between disciplines.
Data sources are suggestions and still need to be verified before relying on them.

### Good first projects (open data, quick to start)

| Idea | The question | Likely data |
|---|---|---|
| **Street-name demographics** | Who do streets honor (gender, race, era, colonial or Confederate figures), and how do renaming debates play out? | OpenStreetMap street names plus public biographical records |
| **Who gets to sit down?** | How much public seating is there per capita and along walking routes, and how does it vary with age, disability, and income? | OpenStreetMap, city asset inventories, a field audit |
| **Geography of waiting** | Where do people wait for buses, benefits, and clinics, and what do those spots offer (shelter, shade, seating, safety)? | Transit stop data (GTFS), city facility lists, field observation |
| **Soundscape and gentrification** | How does what a neighborhood *sounds* like change as it changes (construction, vendors, bells, children)? | 311 noise complaints, public audio archives |

### More ideas

- **Urban smell mapping:** who lives with sewage, rendering plants, or cannabis odors, and who gets to complain?
- **Nighttime cities:** lighting, night-shift transit access, and how safe the night feels by neighborhood.
- **Death and the city:** cemetery land use, cremation trends, and roadside memorials.
- **Ghost infrastructure:** unfinished highways, closed stations, and orphaned plazas. What predicts failure, and does reuse work?
- **Urban wildlife as a stakeholder:** camera-trap and community-science data (iNaturalist) across income lines.
- **Informal city in rich countries:** unpermitted units, garage conversions, street vending, ad-hoc parking markets.
- **Elevators and the vertical city:** subway elevator outages and who is trapped when they fail.
- **Loneliness geography:** free places to be alone or together (libraries, plazas, laundromats) against indicators of isolation.
- **Delivery-economy footprints:** how ghost kitchens, dark stores, and delivery bikes reshape curbs and commercial streets.
- **Teenagers and the city:** where 13-18-year-olds can legally hang out, and what happens when they cannot.

### Housing and transit follow-ups

- **Transit access vs. home values:** distance to stations against census-tract home values, using a new line as a natural experiment. Property-level sales data (TRREB) and bulk assessment data (MPAC) are licensed, so aggregates and census data are the realistic route.
- **Multiplex reform as a natural experiment:** did permitting rules allowing multiplexes citywide change what gets built? This pairs naturally with the Phase 2 pipeline below.
- **Downtown recovery:** foot traffic across cities and what explains the differences.
- **Legacy of redlining:** historical maps against today's health, wealth, and environmental burdens.
- **Heat-island equity:** tree canopy and surface materials against heat-related ER visits, by income and race.
